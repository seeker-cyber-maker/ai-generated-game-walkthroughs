-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[03.01] THE FOUR STUDENTS & KITCHEN TABLE FACTORY                       [HIS01]
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

In 2000, four recent graduates from Imperial College London (Chris Delay,
Mark Morris, John Austin, and Thomas Arundel) formed Introversion Software with
virtually zero funding. Chris programmed Uplink on a lone PC in his spare
bedroom.

When Uplink released on October 8, 2001, it ignited an instant grassroots
phenomenon across the internet. Having no distributor, the four founders turned
their parents' kitchen table into a fulfillment factory. They spent sixteen
hours a day hand-burning CD-Rs, folding black cardboard jewel cases, printing
sticky labels on inkjet printers, and hauling heavy sacks of parcels to the
local post office every afternoon, dubbing themselves "The Last of the Bedroom
Programmers."

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[03.02] STRATEGY FIRST BETRAYAL & LIQUIDATION THREAT                    [HIS02]
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Seeking to bring Uplink to North American retail shelves, Introversion signed a
distribution deal with Canadian publisher Strategy First to release "Uplink:
Hacker Elite" across Best Buy, CompUSA, Walmart, and GameStop in 2003.

Strategy First manufactured and sold tens of thousands of copies across the US.
However, shortly after taking delivery of the master gold discs and collecting
retail revenue, Strategy First filed for Canadian bankruptcy protection (CCAA)
and refused to pay Introversion tens of thousands of pounds in accrued
royalties.

With their capital locked in international bankruptcy proceedings, Introversion
was left with empty bank accounts, mounting debts, and an incomplete prototype
of their next game (Darwinia). Liquidation seemed inevitable.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[03.03] SELLING THE C++ SOURCE CODE: THE DEVELOPER CD                   [HIS03]
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Rather than selling their IP to a conglomerate, Introversion made an audacious,
unprecedented gamble in late 2002/2003: The Uplink Developer CD.

They packaged the entire, un-redacted commercial C++ source code of Uplink,
alongside debug tools, graphics assets, and prototype builds, and sold it
directly to fans for 30 pounds.

The gamble succeeded beyond all expectations:
  1. Financial Rescue: Thousands of fans bought the Developer CD, clearing the
     studio's debts and funding Darwinia (2005) and DEFCON (2006).
  2. Modding Renaissance: Opening the source code birthed legendary community
     overhauls like UplinkOS, The FBI Mod, and modern high-res engine ports.
  3. The Road to Triumph: This fierce commitment to independence paved the way
     for their multi-million-copy masterpiece, Prison Architect!

[04.01] IPV4 GENERATION ALGORITHM                                       [NET01]
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

In Introversion's C++ source engine (world.cpp), pseudo-IP addresses are
generated using the following routine:

char* World::GenerateIP() {
    int octet1 = 12 + (rand() % 220);
    int octet2 = 1 + (rand() % 254);
    int octet3 = 1 + (rand() % 254);
    sprintf(ip, "%d.%d.%d", octet1, octet2, octet3);
    return strdup(ip);
}
