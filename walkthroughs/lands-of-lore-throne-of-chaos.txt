[01.00] AUTHOR'S PREFACE & RESEARCH PHILOSOPHY                          [PREF]
===============================================================================

While this document was assembled and mathematically verified with the
assistance of modern AI tooling and binary disassembly pipelines, it is born
from a deep, lifelong love for classic gaming. The author spent countless
cherished hours playing, mapping, and loving these games as a kid, teenager,
adult, and even yesterday.

While traditional narrative walkthroughs and secrets have been known for
decades, approaching them today from the perspective of an AI Cybersecurity
Researcher and Reverse-Engineer offers a new dimension of appreciation. By
peering directly beneath the hood into decompiled assembly, memory
registers, and state-machine bytecode, we can finally understand--
deterministically and mathematically--what made these cherished old games
tick, every single tick.

[08.00] THE MANUALS, LORE BOOKS, FEELIES & COPY PROTECTION              [MANL]
===============================================================================

A. THE PLAYER'S GUIDE & 48-PAGE RULEBOOK:
  The boxed release contained a 48-page booklet explaining Gladstone politics,
  character creation, weapon mechanics, and the 4 spell disciplines.

B. "THE HISTORY OF THE LANDS" (NARRATIVE FEELIE):
  An illustrated narrative book included with the game detailing the ancestry
  of King Richard Leene, the ancient races (Huline, Dracoids, Thomgogs), the
  Draracle, and Scotia discovering the Nether Mask in the Dark Army ruins.

C. OFF-DISK COPY PROTECTION (FLOPPY VS CD-ROM):
  • 1993 Floppy Edition: Before leaving Gladstone Keep, Chancellor Geron
    presents a manual question: "Turn to Page X, Line Y, enter the Z-th word."
  • 1994 CD-ROM "Talkie" Edition (v1.23): Westwood removed the copy protection
    prompt completely, relying on the physical 600MB CD-ROM as DRM.

D. HISTORICAL ASSEMBLY DECOMPILATION: BYPASSING 90S MANUAL DRM:
  In x86 DOS binaries, manual checks evaluated a conditional jump:
    CALL CheckWordMatch        ; Returns AX=1 (Valid), AX=0 (Invalid)
    TEST AX, AX
    JNZ  PassProtection        ; Machine Code: 75 0C (Jump if Non-Zero)
  Crackers historically bypassed this via 3 methods:
    1. Forced Jump: Patching 75 0C (JNZ) to EB 0C (JMP Short).
    2. NOP Fallthrough: Patching 74 08 (JZ Fail) to 90 90 (NOP NOP).
    3. Function Stubbing: Overwriting entry with B0 01 C3 (MOV AL, 1; RET).
